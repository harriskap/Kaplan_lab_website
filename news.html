Papers, talks, awards, and milestones from the Kaplan Lab.

May 2026 Lab Opening

Kaplan Lab opens at the University of Virginia

Harris Kaplan joins the UVA Department of Biology as Assistant Professor. The lab is located in Gilmer Hall and is actively recruiting graduate students and postdoctoral researchers interested in developmental neuroscience and hypothalamic circuits. If you are interested in joining, please see the Join Us page.

March 2026 Seminar

Neuroscience Seminar at Mount Sinai

Harris presented work on infant brain circuits and hypothalamic development at the Neuroscience Seminar Series at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, New York.

January 2026 Seminar

Stanford Biology Seminar: "A coming-of-age story"

Harris gave an invited talk in the Stanford Biology Department Seminar Series, presenting new work on the neuronal control of behavior in early life under the title "A coming-of-age story: neuronal control of behavior in early life."

December 2025 Preprint

New preprint: brain-immune interactions and sickness states

A new collaborative preprint from the Dulac lab on bioRxiv: Sullivan, Wong, Kaplan, et al. show that brain-immune interactions generate pathogen-specific sickness states, linking immune signals to defined neural circuits.

December 2025 Preprint

New preprint: hormonal signaling and parenting circuits

Logeman, Horvath, Kaplan, Dulac et al. post a new preprint on bioRxiv showing that cell type-specific hormonal signaling configures hypothalamic circuits for parenting, identifying a molecular logic by which hormones tune circuit states.

November 2025 Publication

Perspective published in Nature: the new frontier in brain development

Nowakowski, Nano, Matho, Kaplan et al. contribute a perspective piece to a special Nature issue on brain development, outlining new technologies and open questions in understanding human and mammalian brain development at single-cell resolution. Read →

August 2025 Publication

Paper published in Nature: hypothalamic cell type development

Kaplan, Logeman, Zhang, Yawitz, Dulac et al. publish a molecular-developmental atlas of the hypothalamic preoptic area in Nature. Using single-cell RNA sequencing combined with functional recordings, the study demonstrates that sensory input during early postnatal life, biological sex, and ongoing neuronal activity jointly shape cell-type identity in this brain region — revealing how experience is written into the molecular architecture of a homeostatic circuit. Read →

January 2025 Publication

Review published in Physiological Reviews: neurobiology of parenting

Kaplan, Horvath, Rahman, and Dulac publish a comprehensive review of the neurobiology of parenting and infant-evoked aggression in Physiological Reviews. The review covers circuit mechanisms of parental care across mammals, the hormonal regulation of parenting, and the puzzling phenomenon of infant-directed aggression. Read →

February 2024 Publication

Methods paper in Cell Reports Methods: long-term calcium imaging in pigeons

Nimpf, Nordmann, Cushion, Kaplan, and Keays describe new techniques for long-term, high-resolution in vivo calcium imaging in freely moving pigeons, enabling study of navigation-related neural activity across extended behavioral sessions. Read →

January 2020 Publication

Paper published in Neuron: nested dynamics and behavioral hierarchy

Kaplan, Thula, Khoss, and Zimmer publish in Neuron, demonstrating that nested neuronal dynamics operating across multiple timescales orchestrate a behavioral hierarchy in C. elegans. This work established a framework for understanding how global brain states encode and switch between behaviors. Read →

October 2015 Publication

Paper published in Cell: global brain dynamics in C. elegans

Kato, Kaplan, Schrödel, Skora, Lindsay, Yemini, Lockery, and Zimmer publish in Cell, showing that brain-wide neural dynamics in the nematode C. elegans embed the motor command sequence underlying locomotion. This paper introduced the concept that even a small nervous system uses global, high-dimensional dynamics to generate behavior. Read →
