Principal Investigator
HK

Harris Kaplan

Assistant Professor of Biology

Department of Biology, University of Virginia

Harris grew up in New Jersey and studied biology at New York University, where he worked in Justin Blau's lab on the neuronal circuits that generate circadian rhythms in Drosophila. This introduced him to the idea that specific, genetically defined neurons have precise functions — a thread that has run through his career ever since.

For his PhD at the IMP Vienna with Manuel Zimmer, Harris developed whole-brain imaging techniques in the transparent nematode Caenorhabditis elegans and used them to discover how brain-wide neural dynamics encode ongoing behavior. This work produced two high-profile studies in Cell (2015) and Neuron (2020) and established that global, nested neural dynamics — not local circuits alone — orchestrate behavioral sequences.

As an HHMI Fellow of the Jane Coffin Childs Fund and postdoctoral researcher in Catherine Dulac's lab at Harvard, Harris shifted his focus from invertebrates to the mouse hypothalamus, drawn by the question of how this ancestral brain region drives the most fundamental of all mammalian behaviors: parental care. He developed new approaches to study the developing hypothalamus using single-cell genomics combined with in vivo recordings, culminating in the 2025 Nature paper showing that sensory experience, sex, and neuronal function jointly shape hypothalamic cell-type identity during postnatal development.

The Kaplan Lab at UVA continues this work, asking how infant-specific circuits control early-life behavior, how experience shapes the developing hypothalamus, and what the hundreds of uncharacterized cell types in this brain region actually do.
